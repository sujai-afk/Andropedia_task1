    Entropy is estimated as length × log₂(pool size), where pool size is the sum of character sets detected in your password (lowercase 26, uppercase 26, digits 10, special ~32). This assumes a random password from that pool — real passwords with dictionary words or patterns are weaker than their entropy number suggests, which is why the score above also checks for patterns separately.

    Assumptions: "offline" models an attacker who has stolen a password hash and brute-forces it locally on modern hardware (~10 billion guesses/sec). "Online" models an attacker guessing through a live login form with basic rate-limiting (~100 guesses/sec). Both assume the attacker has to try, on average, half of the total possible combinations (2^entropy ÷ 2) and doesn't already know the password is a common/dictionary word — if it is, real crack time is seconds, regardless of length.
